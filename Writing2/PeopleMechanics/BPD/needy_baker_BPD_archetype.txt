Alright. Im gonna talk about Mid-To-High Functioning BPD people.

BPD, as a concept, is a personality disorder characterized by sharp mindset shifts, hot-and-cold behaviour, difficulty in staying grounded, trust issues and a very, very, very weak sense of identity that drives the previously mentioned symptoms.

People with BPD pattersns tend to strongly attach their value to the perceptions of others, have fractured senses-of-self, difficulty in self assertion/advocacy and subconsciously get in situations that reinforce their deeply rooted beliefs that the world is inherently an unstable place of relentless conflict.

These patterns come associated with anxious attachment styles characterized by eager obcession over some(one/thing) that preceeds immediate detachment from their personally attributed value of that very some(one/thing).

One of the ways this manifests as is the "needy baker" BPD archetype.
These people are often sharp, immensly self-aware, hyper sensitive and hate staying still.
The correlation of it with ADHD and Autism is also noteworthy.
These people live for the craft. When the needy baker tornado cloud is done forming, one of the first insecurities that will sprout in the mind of a needy baker is wether or not they have a talent. because, if they do, they will immediatly lean into it and start leveraging it in order to externalize their sense of value into tangible things that people can see, because they have internalized that their value comes from what they can do and not from who they are.
They are high-powered, restless and if some hearts have very thorny roads one needs to cross to get to them, only for one to be disappointed when they get there, the heart of the needy baker is immense, tender and the road to it isnt thorny or even muddy: Its an intracontinental highway with 5 lanes. That road... is their craft.
They dont shut up about it.
Until they find out what their "Talent" is suposed to be, they spend their lives highly anxious, neurotic and create petty conflicts in their heads out of boredom to fill the void in their soul.
The smallest ammout of social feedback that does not refer directly to their craft tends to leave them unstable, flustered (in a sort of "I love this but it distracts me from what I really think is important" way) and distracted for hours on end after the fact. 
if they get a compliment that isnt about their craft, they will almost always either:
1- Brush it off, to try and keep themselves from spiraling into emotional ping-ponging
2- become immensly enthralled by the concept of themselves that was presented to them, trying as hard as they can to embody it, eventually figure out that they cannot directly associate their identity with a pathwork cloth of comments... because that would mean, for it to be fair, they would also have to directly embed... criticism into their identity as well.
If they get a criticism that isnt about their craft, they do one of 2 things:
1- if the criticism is ill willed, they will lash out. its as simple as that. They will sort of "throw their craft in your face", uttering identity based statements that are verbalized as being about the craft... when they are really not:
"WHAT DO YOU MEAN IM UGLY?! I DONT SEE YOU MAKE THE STUFF I DID! I WORK SO HARD! YOU DONT GET IT! NO ONE GETS IT! FUCK OFF! I HATE YOU!"
2- if the criticism is well intentioned, at their worst, they will shell up, pretend it was never said in the first place... and will very likely not follow it through in the moment. Here is what they do instead:
2.1- If they have a strong enough identity (very, very rare), they will try to Turtle through the hail bombing of negative emotion in order to keep themselves stable, while simultaneously and gradually integrating the *actually* useful part of the criticism into their craft. Very, very, very high percentages of the criticism are allocated towards their craft in the expense of almost everything else. The people who utter these criticisms to them will start being seen as "dangerous mentors that I speak to if I am brave and strong. I must talk to them sometimes otherwise my craft will become crap. I will become crap. So I must be strong."
2.2- Otherwise... they will start following it to the letter right away, in a short-sighted, performative way in order for the author of the critisism to "just go away so I can go back to my craft".

Ive learned how to connect several important things:

The concept of short circuits
The concept of engine power vs wheel power
Lack of cognitive challenge
And me.

So: there is this concept in electronics that is called a short-circuit. a short circuit occurs when a circuit  is switched on without any resistive elements for the kinetic energy of the electrons to dissipate away.
Because Resistance is Voltage (potential) divided by current (lets assume that the entire circuit is ohmic. this is ohms law),

If we switch Resistance with current, we get:

current is voltage divided by resistance.
Which means that... for a constant source of voltage V, if the resistance approaches zero plus, the current approaches infinity plus.

Which is what is happening to me whenever I stay away from challenges that make me struggle for too long. My mind sorta lifts its driven wheels over an appropriately challenging terrain and, in the absense of a rev limiter (my BPD), the wheel rpm just sorta... shoots into space without anything really happening.
When I am writing, My engine is running, yes. but the wheel rpm I am putting out is zero (or close to it) because the feedback I am getting isnt rewarding enough for me. (I am basically mathifying unfulfillment. lol). But as soon as I get a proper challenge, The wheels can no longer spin 100% freely and I can then actually control where I am going!
