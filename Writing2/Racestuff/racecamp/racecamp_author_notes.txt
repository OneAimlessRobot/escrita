But the triad has a shadow.
With growing emphasis on each trait,
a matching shadow forms as well.

For racers that instinctively focus on aggressiveness:
To rank ahead of the rest,
outsmart opponents in duels,
driven to chase down what lies ahead almost as if it taunted you.

That is the essense of this trait.
But.
Over time,
Leaking through the cracks of the self aware persona we all possess,
remnants of ones dominant traits that spill over.

For starters:
Racers focusing on Aggressiveness,
Might display [toxic defensiveness].
In the chase to achieve the highest rank possible,
in a pinch,
one might start favouring attitudes which serve other purpose but to deny the opponent of getting a high rank themselves.

The racers which value aggressiveness will not hesitate to put their car millimeters alongside yours even at the fastest sweeping corners one could imagine
(Aka: Like alonso did to michael shumacker at that race in suzuka,
where alonso overtook the German [by the outside] on the [fastest cornet on the track].
And both were driving fragile,
Twichy, neck breakingly fast, open wheeled formula one race cars.
One touch of a wing on tire,
or even one mistimed correction?
When both were already going around a corner at two hundred kilometers per hour OR MORE,
In vehicles that will throw a spectacular fit at [any] disturbance of fluid flow across their highly specialized aerodynamic bodies?

High!
Aggessiveness!

They go for meter sized gaps invisible to the drivers they attack,
They swerve,
they feint and they cause pressure which creates mistakes in the opposition they face!

They often read the rulebook like it is a bible,
as,
even very early in a season,
in case of a championship where the rules changed in relation the previous year,
They want to add as many grey areas to their arsenal as they can.
And if the rules say the car needs to just [be] on the road at a certain stretch of road to avoid a penalty,
If it guarantees that they can one up the opposition,
They [will] keep [anything] except the outermost mirrors inside the track if it means going as fast as possible at the exit of a corner.

They are obssessive,
and they often rile up the paddock,
injecting the whole team with fire when they are at their best.
They ultimately raise the conpetitiveness among racers,
leading to a higher level racing
egging composed drivers on when the margin gets too wide
and keeping precise teamate drivers from growing too shy inside their boxes.


BUT--
...
[At the expense] of things such as:
Sustainability of pace,
consistency of pace,
Staying focused on the course,
And making enemies through hostile and unecessarily harsh challenges.

One may swerve in front of an incoming opponent who is evidently too fast to be contained in an organic way
preventing them from overtaking even if the car "ahead" is technically a backmarker,
or letting their emotions boil over,
interfering with the trajectory of a racer who could ge going faster,
but is held up by a weaker racer.

Overly aggressive racers also may forget that fighting costs time.
Which ends up penalizing their precision,
which is the ability to maintain raw race pace,
possesses high sensitivity to the behaviour of the car,
through either a specific car setup that they are able to constantly find speed with,
Or simply breezing past opponents in qualifying,
or within a highly spread out field when the road is unfavourable.
They feel deltas in grip at one hundred and ten percent of the normal volume,
Bad exists annoy them
Microscopic slips enrage them,
And excessively precise racers may...
well,
become afraid of getting disrupted
Leading to rigidness,
which is the second trait in the dark triad of racing.
Precise players will complain CONSTANTLY if unregulated and undisciplined: They hate the tire they wore 3 races ago on their third stop,
They make unecessary calls on the radio for worn tires seemingly AGES before engineers would even think about pitting.
They are demanding, and even arrogant towards others who cannot match them in qualifying.
But at the start of races,
they may freezs up.
As all the cars in front of them represent way too many variables for their highly efficient,
yet infrastructure-dependent methods.

They may even end up in situations where,
even despite not being able to apply their usual driving style,
They attempt idealistic lines,
running out of road.
They are penalized for track limits,
getting [even more] frustrated.
And may end up [avoiding] fights,
shying away from approaching slower cars under the pretext of maintaining their "natural flow".

They are technicians.

And may forget that races often take [way] longer than one corner,
and one lap,
and that championships are often longer than one single race.
And professional racing is based on championships comprised of multiple races
where winning everything all the time,
or always being the best possible pure driver does not really matter.

But.
Their sensitivity,
Intuitive knowledge of the car
and their ability to just grow speed even in the harshest machines to grow it on
Is essential to keep the hopes at the paddock up,
Raise the buffer levels of composed racers,
and motivate aggressive ones.

Engineers want their feedback.
And they use their knowledge to,
in the end,
drive innovation forward.

To keep this things in mind,
as a motorsports athlete,
is to maintain [composure].
Races are long!
And winning is impossible to happen before the regulated time limit/distance (plus one lap)
OR the designated number of laps,
if it is so specified,
Is physically impossible.

Driving using the points,
trusting the margins you are given,
pushing when others slack,
and pressing patiently until opponents crack is the essence of composure.
Drivers will [break].
and when they do,
their team has one more hassle to deal with.
Their other drivers feel pressured,
and their aggressiveness may increase.
Which can [also] mean an upcomming stronger challenge for those who wield composure as their main weapon.

Knowing when to wait for bright-burning racers to crash,
spin out,
get greedy in stressful situations,
Or precise drivers get overwhelmed with constant pressure,
or spin out in conditions where their fixated methods dont hand them victories

Is essential to keep the mood at the paddock stable,
and the efforts exerted by the team to win,
sustainable within the budget they have.


But.

Waiting for the train is innevitable.
But if one is not even on the train station to catch it in the first place,
They [will] miss it.
And waiting has given them...
nothing.
In an attempt to handle pressure from multiple sides,
in situations where precise drivers get too good,
or aggressive drivers one up them too often,
They may [fawn].
Composed drivers,
across a long streak of mishandled adversities,
might get the temptation to focus excessively on [effortless survival],
becoming narcissistic, indulging in [nonchallant] mindsets that merely feed an instinct to stay complacent.

And the train example explains it well enough, I feel.
If the race is nearly over,
and the composed driver waits for the perfect opportunity for toi long...
they will lose.
Its as simple as that!

At the paddock,
they serve as an ice bucket for bruised egos.
Desinfectant for wounds of grief.

Keeping aggressive drivers in their place when their reckless risk taking nearly causes a dangerous crash
And keeping precise drivers from becoming too perfectionistic and neurotic.
Assuring them that even if they completed the perfect lap,
It would likely not happen again [anytime] soon.
The same being for any other driver.

And reminding everyone that opponent drivers are not traffic cones,
and that their brains can be used against them, as well.

Well,
that is the dark triad of racing!

And along with it,
we now have a full personality system (I feel like there is):

Aggressiveness - Defensiveness.
Precision - rigidity.
Composure - Complacency.

I call this the tri-axis racing personality system.
