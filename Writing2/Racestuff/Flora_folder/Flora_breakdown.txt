Its a story about a girl named Flora. She is a motorsport pilot that started (and was very successful at) karting, then formula Renault, then formula 3. Then she went to IMSA to race for a privateer team named Gillet, where she would pilot the  Outlandish, innovative and precise Gillet Vertigo at the 12 hours of Laguna Seca, where she would actually get 5th place, due to it being rainy and her sensitive driving instincts being very good for the rain. The car was underpowered but she could actually take the corkscrew and final hairpins faster than anyone else... leading her to, prior to the race, actually get the 5th actual time in the GTE class in qualifying. so... she finished where she started. But not without challenges: Flora suffers from a severe lack of confidence, She was diagnosed with Autism Spectrum Disorder at 10 years old and gets VERY overwhelmed when she isnt alone in the track. She g(ets)ot very overwhelmed in pack-racing situations or when she h(as)ad to overtake some TCR backmarker cars. She also feels very reluctant to take advantage of slipstream... as she turbulence she feels on the chassis and her body makes her "Feel uneasy". Because of her Autism, her emotions get processed in a very diesel-engine like fashion, where, due to being unable to otherwise process the worlds' (In her mind) relentless wave of feedback and stimulations, pushes back on her own needs and bottles them, bottles them, bottles them to then have a meltdown. During these meltdowns, she always tends to exhibit toxic behaviours that range from self-sabotaging to self-destructive, leading to post-meltdown guilt, which compounds her repression. But her condition comes with advantages: She was hand-picked by the Gillet Engineers to pilot the Vertigo, as they saw her performances in Formula 3, where she was starting to get very dominant and so caught their attention, and noticed how, despite not being agressive or going for any divebombs, she was always able to keep milisecond-consistency pace and to conserve hard-compound tires (That, sorta-tragically, she absolutely hates to drive on) so well that they last the whole race... ending up winning due to pit strategy alone, most of the time. The Gillet required precision? She had it. The Gillet required skill to make the most of its less hungry, but also less powered engine combined with the sharper handling and precision? She had it. And also... when it rains... during the IMSA event, she ran laps in the thing that were only 1 to 2 seconds shy of her actual times in dry tarmac! But... a danger was on the rise. Flora, and a few other protagonist characters, were hand chosen by a racing camp that set itself out to do something important: To persistently and actively fight corruption in racing. They would scoop drivers from teams and raise them in their facilities, equipped with anything from dorms, to cafeterias to a dirt track, an american Style oval, a city circuit and a speed-way circuit. All within the premisses. The circuits were all in Croatia. (The SpeedWay circuit was DragonTrail Seaside btw). The drivers were all pitted against eachother and analyzed for their specific skills, MBTI, driving styles and previous experience. They also got prescribed cognitive skill tests and logical quizzes. The premisses have access to medications sponsored by Bayer (Thats how Jessica, another protagonist, gets the medications for her Hallucinations and ADHD and How flora gets Risperidone for her acute anxiety attacks and to smoothen out her emotional processing). Lets focus on Flora's journey: She always tended to be very, very isolated during the camp, often just flocking to the pharmacy to speak with the staff there, as they were all well versed in Florology and chemistry in general, which Flora (Thats where her name comes from) absolutely adores. Flora has short, blonde hair, grey eyes, a neutral expression in her face and very pale skin. When out of tasks She is often seen carrying reserved yet fresh, hand-sewn clothing mixed with umbro sneakers and nothing in the way of accessories. She had the best eyesight in the entire scouted sample of that year. After 3 years in the camp, she is, currently 1.64 meters tall and weighs 72 kg. When she entered she was 1.56meters tall and weighed at only 59kg. She needed to bulk up more if she wanted to be given a proper chance against other drivers, as she technically only got 5th at the laguna seca race due to lots of cautions and because the final stretch had lots of rain. According to the race camp's psychological theory of "The holy trinity of the pilot mind", Her score in that test at the beginning was: "10% aggression, 80% precision and 10% composure". Now... it is "20% aggression, 60% precision and 20% composure". She also has an interesting detail: Her inner child, to whom she unwillingly delegates all her emotional processing, due to being unable to do any of it by herself, presents a sensational, dramatic, sassy and bitter personality and it represents all her complaints, distaste and even genuine anger that she feels towards the world but never expresses. And it basically is what "detonates" whenever she has a melt down. When she got scouted, she was initially going to be mapped to the Williams F1 team in F1. But... when they saw, while testing the drivers in 2013 machinery (due to lack of access to any hybrid cars), that, although her telemetry data was extremely promising, the social side of her racing skills were severely limiting. And also... she rarely complained to her engineers even if she needed help or hated the setup, almost always resigning herself to just "work with it". So... they got her in a meeting with the F1 division director a the racing camp scout who saw her, Kafka right? Between the lines, she was told: "Look. You would serve us way better if you drove... in the WRC.". And, for the first time in her racing career, she actually spoke her mind. She said, and I quote: "Oh... my god! What a relief! I genuinely thought I was gonna have to drive an F1 car! And have an engineer scream at me for the rest of my LIFE! Thank god! [Sighs deeply]".
